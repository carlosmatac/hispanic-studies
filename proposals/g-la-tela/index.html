Cross of Burgundy · 1506–1843 · Flanders → Florida → Manila → Potosí
203K views · our most-watched Short

Proposal B's flag, now real fabric.

His most-viewed Short (203K) is about the Cross of Burgundy, and the 151K one about Bolivians waving it. Cloth Physics hangs it as a heavy banner in the wind and the cursor pushes the folds: the visitor touches the flag that flew from Flanders to Florida, Manila and Potosí. The most "wow" per effort, and the same scene can carry the tilma of Guadalupe for a devotional page (gently, no glitch). The flag is also used by far-right groups in Spain: ask Beltrán how he wants to handle it on merch.

Effect stack

  1. Image layer: the Cross of Burgundy (Wikimedia Commons, public domain) on burgundy velvet
  2. Cloth Physics in media mode: pinned at the top, strong wind, the cursor repels the fabric
  3. Vignette in burgundy-black, Film Grain
  4. Next: Glyph Dither on top for the castle.tech look, or the tilma of Guadalupe as the fabric

How it is built

Scene "G · La Tela" in the Unicorn Studio design "IHS · Retablo": an image layer under a Cloth Physics effect that maps the layers below onto the fabric. Embedded with <div data-us-project> + the SDK (v2.4.2); Free plan watermark until we publish from a paid account. Headless Chrome (software WebGL) renders the cloth black; check it in a real browser.

Without Unicorn

Verlet cloth (the three.js cloth example) with the flag as texture and mouse picking. A GPU cloth like Unicorn's is much harder.
