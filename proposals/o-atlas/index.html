One civilization, two languages, four continents.

Spanish and Portuguese are official in twenty-nine states on four continents. That space has a history, an intellectual tradition and a two-hundred-year argument about what it is. This is the reference for it.

I

Three trunks

Everything here hangs off one of three.

Mestizaje, Catholicism and language. The three pillars are how the videos, the essays, the books and the canon are organised, not a slogan.

Mestizaje

Castas, transculturación, La raza cósmica, Indo-American thought, and the Black Legend on race.

Series: Race, Mixture and Difference · Indo-Hispanic Thought and Its Critics — 8 priority titles

Catholicism

Guadalupe, evangelisation, the School of Salamanca, and Catholic political thought on both sides of the Atlantic.

Series: Classics and Critics of Hispanidad · Hispanic Political Theology — 6 priority titles

Language

Spanish and Portuguese, the academies, Iberofonía, and translation as the work of the publishing house itself.

Series: Pan-Iberian and Lusophone Thought · Literary Continentalism — 14 priority titles

III

The canon · fifty texts, 1818 to 1999

A living argument, not a reading list.

Every text is placed by the kind of unity it presupposes. Hispanists have been monarchists and Marxists, liberals and nationalists; the point is the dispute, which is why the authors who argued against Hispanic unity are on the chart too, drawn with a broken outline.

Point at a text to read it.

Fifty works, eight positions, one hundred and eighty years.

Classification adapted from Gustavo Bueno, “España y América” (2001). Portuguese, Brazilian, Lusophone African, Asian, Indigenous, Black Atlantic, Equatoguinean and Filipino traditions remain historically distinct. Their presence on this chart does not imply their assent to a Hispanic or Iberophone project.

IV

De Indiis Editions

Translating a canon, not a bookshelf.

Fifty priority acquisitions across thirteen series. Most of these works were read widely in their day and were never translated. Each edition places its text inside the tradition it came from.

Ranks are editorial recommendations from the acquisitions programme, not publication announcements. Proposed introducers and editors are not listed: they are scholars who have not agreed to participate. Covers are placeholders.

VII

The mission

A tradition that was forgotten, not refuted.

The Institute for Hispano Studies studies and defends Hispanic civilization: its history, its Catholic inheritance, its mestizaje and its two languages. De Indiis Editions translates and republishes the canon of that argument.

The claim is not that this tradition was defeated. It is that most of it was never translated, and that an argument you cannot read is an argument you cannot answer.
