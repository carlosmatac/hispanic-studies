Talavera de la Reina → Puebla · UNESCO intangible heritage, 2019
Move the cursor to glaze the tiles

A shared craft as a pattern system.

Talavera crossed from Talavera de la Reina to Puebla and stayed: in 2019 UNESCO inscribed it jointly for Spain and Mexico. That is his thesis in ceramic form, a culture that travelled and took root rather than a loot. The tile is also a system: the same eagle-and-cross module tiles headers, section breaks, packaging and the shop, and every visitor finishes the wall by glazing it.

Effect stack

  1. Tile module drawn in code: the IHS eagle (logo mask) and the Cross of Burgundy alternate in the centre, corner quarter-rosettes join across four tiles
  2. Bisque → glaze: tiles start as fired clay with the pencil sketch, the cursor glazes them in cobalt on tin-white (a radial wipe from where you touch)
  3. Wet sheen that follows the cursor; burgundy in the saltires, like Talavera's manganese
  4. Unicorn version: Recursive Grid + mouse ripple + Bloom on the glaze

Merch spin-off

Real tiles, mugs and plates from a Puebla workshop, with the eagle-and-cross module; the pattern also wraps the shop's packaging.

Built with our own code

Canvas 2D in main.js: four pre-rendered tile faces (two motifs × bisque/glazed), a glaze state per tile and a clip circle for the wipe. Light theme on purpose: the only daylight direction among D–K. With reduced motion the wall is shown fully glazed and still.
