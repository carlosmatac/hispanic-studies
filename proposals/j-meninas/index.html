Diego Velázquez · Las Meninas · 1656 · Museo del Prado
Move the cursor: the room moves with you

Who is looking at whom?

Las Meninas is a painting about the viewer: the king and queen stand where we stand, seen only in the mirror. A slight parallax makes that literal, as if the visitor stepped into the Alcázar. It also suits an institute of ideas: Velázquez painting himself at work is the intellectual as part of the court of a universal monarchy.

Effect stack

  1. Room: the painting itself, blurred and scaled up, as a dim background layer
  2. Canvas: Las Meninas (Prado, public domain) in a frame, tilting a few degrees towards the cursor
  3. Glint: a soft highlight that slides across the varnish against the movement
  4. Vignette as a front layer moving the most; three depths give the illusion
