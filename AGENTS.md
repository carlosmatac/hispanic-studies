## Start of every session (do this before writing anything)

1. Who am I working for? `gh api user --jq .login`.
2. Sync: `git fetch origin --prune` and `git status`. If you are on a branch, merge main into it (see "Staying in sync").
3. Context: read `ROADMAP.md`, then the open work:
   - `gh issue list --assignee @me --state open`
   - `gh pr list --state open` (what the other person has in flight)
   - `git log --oneline origin/main -15` (what landed recently)
4. Pick up the issue the user asks for, or the lowest-numbered open issue assigned to them whose dependencies are closed. The issue's checklist is the scope. Skip `parked` issues.
5. Keep the PR to the issue's scope. Shared pieces (`site/lib/`, `site/app/globals.css`, the datasets) are everyone's: say in the PR what you changed there and why.

## Git workflow (mandatory)

- **Never commit to `main` directly.** Every change goes through a branch and a pull request.
- **One issue = one branch = one PR.** Branch from the latest `origin/main`:
  `git switch -c <type>/<issue>-<slug> origin/main`, e.g. `feat/42-canon-page`, `fix/60-map-labels`, `docs/47-meeting-notes`.
  Types: `feat`, `fix`, `chore`, `docs`, `refactor`.
- Commit small and often, with clear messages (`canon: jump to a neighbour from the panel`). Push early: `git push -u origin HEAD`, so the other person can see the work.
- Open the PR as soon as there is something to look at (draft is fine): `gh pr create --fill --draft`, then fill in the template. The body must include `Closes #<issue>`.
- Before marking the PR ready: merge main in again and check the pages in the browser (see "Verification").
- **Merging**: squash merge (`gh pr merge --squash --delete-branch`).
  - PRs that only touch one section of the site (or docs) can be merged by their author.
  - PRs touching shared pieces (`site/lib/`, `site/app/globals.css`, datasets, `AGENTS.md`) get the `area:shared` label and need the other person's approval.
- Never force-push to `main`. Never rewrite someone else's branch. Never commit secrets.

## Staying in sync with the other person's work

There is no staging environment: `main` is the only place our work meets. **Integrate early and often:**

- Merge main into your branch at the start of every session, before opening or updating a PR, and whenever the other person merges something you depend on: `git fetch origin && git merge origin/main`
- Use merge, not rebase, on branches that are already pushed.
- Resolve conflicts keeping both people's intent. If a conflict touches work the other person is doing, ask before choosing a side.
- Keep PRs small so they land fast.

## Issues are the documentation

- Every piece of work has an issue with a checklist. Decisions, links, screenshots and open questions go in issue comments: `gh issue comment <n> --body "..."`.
- Anything that depends on Beltrán goes in an issue labelled `parked` (or `meeting` for meeting input/output) instead of being built on a guess.
- After the meeting, write the notes in `docs/meeting/` and turn every decision into an issue.

Labels: `area:site`, `area:data`, `area:brand`, `area:shared`, `area:research`, `area:infra`, `meeting`, `parked`.

- **Colours and fonts** are tokens at the top of `site/app/globals.css`. Four families of muted historical-atlas hues, a shade per realm and its name on the map: **Spain and its realms in reds** anchored in burgundy (`es.spain` #a5464a for Castile and Spain; red #c26450, rose #d2949d, wine #85415e, brick #bb7b4e, amber #d1af70, plum #97648b, coral #d3896c), **Portugal and its realms in greens and teals** (`pt.portugal` #509779; brazil #66aea2, sage #99c199, teal #488c94, pine #397664), **al-Andalus and the Islamic states in blues and violets** (`isl.andalus` #61629b; steel #728ab4, violet #9e78af, sky #94a0c8, slate #584d78), **Rome, the Visigoths and the Sueves in stone** (stone #9f907b, ochre #91745d, sand #b5aa8f, clay #8b6959, olive #5f6042), and **the independent states that followed the Spanish and Portuguese realms in pale counterparts of their shades** (`INDEPENDENT` in the palette: peach #e8c7a9, mauve #bf8f9d, lilac #c3b3d1, sand #ad9c7f, straw #e2dcb7, heather #9b9bba, blush #dcb3a8, mist #bae0e0, mint #d2e1c5, clay #c29e95); during the wars of independence a realm keeps its shade, hatched. Which realm wears which shade is curated per family (`tint` in `site/data/atlas-eras.json` and `tools/atlas-realms.json`) so neighbours differ. - **Rounded, and glass where something floats** (Carlos, 2026-10-30, #89, after a reference for the Atlas): every corner comes from the radius tokens in `globals.css`: `--radius-s` (8px: inputs, covers, chips), `--radius-m` (14px: pictures, panels, menus), `--radius-l` (22px: cards, dialogs, the plates), `--radius-pill` (buttons, switches, badges, bars); round icon buttons are circles. Over a dark veil (the record dialog) the sheet stays solid paper. Full-bleed pieces stay square: the title page's glass, the reel, the fresco band, the epigraph's valley, the footer. Sober, not bubbly: no new colours, no gradients beyond the lit edge.

- **The footer** (since 2026-11-23, issue #106, after algoreto.com's footer; the 2026-10-26 blind, a frosted glass lifted over Carlos's HISPANO STUDIES picture, is gone): one burgundy panel. On top, the links as a grid of cells parted by hairlines (the eagle; Explore, Channels, Community; the motto and blurb above the sources) on the plain ground; under them, a band where **our generated film plays as pixels**: a Spanish tercio on the march (the pikes against the low sun, the helmets under the banner) and a ship off the Pillars of Hercules (`site/public/video/footer-film.mp4`, 17 s, 247 KB, cut from `assets/video/tercio-1080.mp4` and `columnas-hercules-loop.mp4`; credited under Sources as a generated video, never as a record). The film is quantised live to a ramp of the brand's dark colours at low contrast and dissolves into the ground at the band's top edge, pixel by pixel; the bottom line (© Hispano Studies LLC, the version, "Made by algoreto" with its logo, linking to https://algoreto.com) sits on a veil of the ground. The subject must stay readable through the pixels (Carlos rejected an abstract texture of plans and maps the same day: nobody could tell what it was), and the footer must stay quiet. `site/components/FooterPixels.tsx` draws each frame into a canvas of one pixel per cell, picks a ramp step per cell by luminance (eased auto levels, a 4×4 ordered dither) and lets CSS scale it up (`image-rendering: pixelated`): Canvas 2D on the CPU, no WebGL, the screen's DPR costs nothing. It loads nothing until the footer is near, plays only while it is on screen and the tab is visible, redraws at most 12 times a second; reduced motion, Save-Data or a film that fails show the still (`footer-film.jpg`) pixelated once. **Every knob** (cell size and its phone size, ramp, contrast, levels window, dither, speed, fps, veil, focus) is in `site/lib/footer-pixels.ts`; the band's height is `.foot__field` in `globals.css`. The ground (`--foot-ground`) is the ramp's own middle. Only brand colours and mixes of them.
- **Deploys**: Vercel project `hispanostudies-preview` (team `carlos-projects-3203b073`), connected to this GitHub repo since 2026-11-22: Root Directory `site`, framework Next.js, production branch `main`. **Every merge to `main` deploys https://hispanostudies-preview.vercel.app by itself**; every pushed branch/PR gets its own preview URL behind the same password. No more `vercel deploy` by hand (the project root is `site`, so a CLI upload from inside `site/` fails). Previews for Beltrán sit behind a password (Vercel's own password protection is a paid add-on): whenever the `SITE_PASSWORD` env var is set, `site/proxy.ts` sends visitors to the entry page at `/enter` (`site/app/enter/`, issue #86: the title page's shapes and glass, one password field, EN/ES), which sets a 30-day httpOnly cookie holding an HMAC of an expiry keyed on the password (`site/lib/preview-gate.ts`), then goes on to where the visitor was going. Without it, pages redirect there and anything else (videos, data, server actions) gets 401; the pictures in `public/art` and `public/brand` stay open (the image optimizer fetches them without the cookie). Changing `SITE_PASSWORD` signs everyone out. It is not the back office's sign-in (`/admin` has its own accounts). Locally, without the variable, the site is open. Because merging deploys, merge to `main` only what is verified.

## The archive (`/[lang]/archive`, issues #68, #69 and #93)

- **The curated 80** (Beltrán, 2026-11-22, #93): **"De la Monarquía Universal a la iberofonía: 80 textos sobre nuestra unidad civilizatoria" / "From Universal Monarchy to Iberofonía: 80 Texts on Civilizational Unity"** is now the archive's landing. The package he sent (the brief, the JSON, the edition inventory, the drafts) is the source of record in `docs/research/iberofonia/`; read its `DEI_Iberofonia_Developer_Brief.md` before touching the archive. Six **eras** (ES/EN names; keep "Iberofonía" in English copy and the exact lowercase "iberofonía" in the Spanish title); the works in Beltrán's (chronological) order; browse by era, region (his ten, several per work, `browse_regions`) or reading language, and search by author, title, variant title and issuing institution. **No theoretical labels or filters** (the compiler's camp/Bueno class are not shown for the 80). The archive is **free and without registration** (no user system on the site; paid content goes on Substack; Patreon gating, #72, is cancelled).
- **A work of the 80** has a header (original title; author or issuing body; date and its kind; scope), an **article** in Spanish and English (about the author or issuing body; the work in its time; why read it today: `work_articles`), and **editions** (`editions`) grouped by **reading language** (ES, PT, EN; originals in Latin, Italian, Nahuatl… are credited under "Sources", never as empty tabs). The ES/EN article switch is independent of the reading editions. Each edition has an access state (`extent`), and the button the brief's table gives it (`site/lib/editions.ts`): "Read at <source>" for a complete text online, "Publisher edition", "Request access" or "Edition record" otherwise; **never a false "full text" or "PDF download"**. Companion documents are listed apart, never as editions. When no complete English translation is known (`english_state`), the page shows the brief's appeal, linking to the Collaborate page with `?work=<slug>`; never on Oliveira Martins (English left out on purpose).
- **Review**: every article came in **in review**; signed-in editors see it on the site, marked; it is public once approved at **`/admin/curated`** (per work, or a whole era). A work we had before the 80 keeps its old record public until then.
- **The import**: `npm run iberofonia:import` (dry run and manifest by default; `--apply` to write). It upserts by Beltrán's `canon_id` (DEI-C001…): 32 of the 80 were already in our archive and were connected by hand (`MERGES` in `site/scripts/iberofonia-import.mts`), keeping their id, address, `#T030` links, record and texts; the rest are new works whose id is Beltrán's `slug`. `/archive/<slug>` redirects to `/archive/<id>` when they differ. Running it again changes nothing; it never undoes edits made at `/admin` (`--overwrite` to take a new JSON, history kept). A work that leaves the 80 keeps its record and address.
- **The canon became an archive of Hispanic thought** (Beltrán, 2026-10-29: it matters more than the Atlas): a reference site where people come to read the primary sources of the great debates of the Hispanic world. Public-domain texts are readable in full, comfortably on a phone (most reading will be on phones, and long reading will carry AdSense later, #70). Texts still under copyright are **never hosted**: their page is the record plus links to legitimate copies only (publisher, library, Internet Archive lending), never unlicensed PDFs.
- **It lives in a database** (since 2026-10-29, #69; Carlos: no GitHub for Beltrán): Neon Postgres on Vercel, through Drizzle (`site/lib/db/schema.ts`, migrations in `site/db/migrations/`). A work is a record (title, author, year, section, region, language, rights, source edition/URL/revision/licence, links, the compiler's camp/position/Bueno class, draft or published), its texts (Markdown, one per language: Spanish and/or English, and the original language when it is another; shown only when the work is in the U.S. public domain; reading time computed on save), its references (formatted as citations by `lib/cite.ts`) and its history (a snapshot before every change). The sections (periods), the regions and the archive page's own words are in the database too. The site reads it in `site/lib/archive.ts` (static pages, cache tag `archive`); the shared shapes and the languages are in `site/lib/canon.ts`. The fifty works from the compiler keep their ids (`T001`…`T050`); new works get readable ids (`las-casas-brevisima`). The Markdown files it came from are in git history (`246fe7e`).
- **Beltrán edits it himself** in the back office at **`/admin`** (user name and password; Spanish, with English): works, texts (a WYSIWYG editor that pastes cleanly from Word and Google Docs, with a preview in the reader's own style), references, sections, regions, the page's words, history and restore, backup. Every save shows on the site at once (no deploy). Accounts are made with `npm run user:create -- <name>`. Everything else, for both of us: `docs/cms.md`. **Never wipe or reimport over the Neon database**: it is shared by the preview and our laptops; tests use throwaway rows or a local PGlite (`npm run db:local`).
- **Rights: U.S. law** (since 2026-11-22, Beltrán's brief, #93; it replaced the earlier rule "public domain in the US, in Spain and in its own country"): the site is served from the U.S., so a full text is hosted when it is in the **U.S. public domain**: in 2026, **published in 1930 or earlier** (the 95-year term), or another documented U.S. basis. Do not derive it from the author's death, the date of composition, a manuscript's age or a scan being free online; analyse each translation, edition, introduction and annotation separately (a 1946 translation of an 1897 work is not free). On a work, `rights: public-domain` and `pd-us-only` may show the text (`HOSTABLE_RIGHTS` in `site/lib/canon.ts`); `pd-outside-us` and `in-copyright` get the record, legitimate links and the year they come free (`pdSpainYear`, `pdUsYear`). An **edition** of the 80 is hosted (as text in the reader, or a PDF in the viewer, stored in Vercel Blob) only with an approval at `/admin` naming its U.S. basis, the evidence, what was checked, who approved it and when, and under the 1930 rule the edition's year: checked when saved and again when shown (`hostingProblems` in `site/lib/editions.ts`). The JSON's `html_candidate` / `pdf_candidate` are a **queue for staff, not permission**; the import never turns hosting on. The nine texts hosted before #93 were re-checked under the new rule (all published by 1930; see #93). Public-domain works that exist online only as scans carry `needsTranscription: true`: they go in once someone has transcribed and proofread them (no raw OCR on the site).
- **Full texts** come in through the back office (typed or pasted), or through `tools/ingest-text.py` (Wikisource: one exact revision; Project Gutenberg; TEI such as ELTeC), which cleans the text to Markdown for `npm run text:import -- <id>` (source fields included). Before ingesting, check the work is in the public domain and the copy is the original text (not a modern translation, not an annotated or abridged edition), and log it in `docs/archive-sources.md`.
- **Periods**: six boxes, dates only (ours): a **prelude** (1492–1799, the Empire's own thought: Vitoria, Las Casas, Sepúlveda, Acosta, the Inca Garcilaso, Guaman Poma, Solórzano, Clavijero, Viscardo), then the compiler's five. Do not add hues outside it. A work goes where its author worked or what it is about; the per-work calls are in `tools/migrate-canon-to-archive.py`. The page shows a legend.
- Authors are named as they are known (the compiler's short names cut many Spanish names wrong: "Miguel y Jugo" is Unamuno); the mapping is in the migration script.

## The Atlas (`site/lib/atlas-chronicle.ts`, `site/lib/atlas-globe.ts`, `site/components/atlas/`, `/[lang]/atlas`)

- Rebuilt on 2026-11-22 (issue #95) and made our own on 2026-11-23 (#102; Carlos found the first app "the same as Prometheus, a blatant copy"). `/[lang]/atlas` is **an app**: the globe fills the window under the site header, the page never scrolls (`html:has(.atlas-app)` in `atlas.css`), the wheel and trackpad zoom toward the pointer, pinch on phones, drag turns. Our own furniture: a chart's **cartouche** top left (chapter, year, the stop's title and one sentence), one **rail of instruments** down the right edge (index, layers, globe/flat, whole screen, how it works, zoom), the **legend on paper** bottom left, a portolan's scale, and the time bar (previous, play the story, next, the timeline by chapter); the card is a side panel that closes (a sheet on phones). Do not bring back what was lifted from Prometheus: "Surprise me", "Places & events", "Map options", "YEAR … CE" with a dotted underline, "Drag to explore · Scroll to zoom", polity labels. The story is guided, as Beltrán asked; it never plays by itself.
- **Layers** (remembered per device): the rest of the inhabited world (one quiet grey, on), realms, borders and treaty lines (on), recorded by the voyages (wakes, landfalls and keyframe jurisdictions lit in paper, on), routes and voyages (on), events = Beltrán's records in force as burgundy lozenges (on), place names (ours only, on), and **"hide what was not yet recorded", the dark fog, off by default**.
- **World polities are out of scope** (Carlos, 2026-11-23, #102): no empire or polity outside the Iberian story is coloured or named. Beltrán's manuscript asks for a muted orientation basemap; nothing is drawn beyond what his records and our OHM keyframes provide. (Cliopatria was tried in #95 and removed.)
- **The text version** is `/[lang]/atlas/chronicle`: every stop as text, by chapter, then the sixty milestones; it reads without JavaScript and the app links to it (Places & events, Map options, noscript). Records in Spanish come from `site/data/atlas-records.es.json` when present (`machine: true` shows "traducción automática, pendiente de revisión").
- **Beltrán's records** (`site/data/atlas-records.json`, 139 records A07–A146): built by `tools/build-atlas-records.py` from `docs/research/atlas-v3/` (never edit the JSON by hand; rerun the parser). The manuscript's import contract rules: stable ids, A01–A06 and A101 retired and never reused; four paragraphs, type, regions, map anchor, Wikipedia, book, further reading and a Commons plate with its role per record; **geometry and effective dates stay null** until evidenced; the Spanish is a separate file, `site/data/atlas-records.es.json` (#100: a machine translation, every card marked "traducción automática, pendiente de revisión" until Beltrán reviews it; never machine-translate silently); Bueno's thesis (A138) is labelled as interpretation. The map anchors are geocoded against `tools/atlas-gazetteer.json` into **approximate camera anchors** (`approx: true`): rings for the camera, drawn over the dark, **never an unlock**. Section 7's child actions are kept as `staging`, not playback. Plates: `tools/fetch-atlas-plates.py` checks each file's licence, author and repository through the Commons API and ships only free ones (`site/public/art/atlas/`, logged in `assets/art/SOURCES.md`).
- **The chronicle** (`lib/atlas-chronicle.ts`, built on the server per language): the records, **ten voyages** (`site/data/atlas-voyages.json`), the **twenty border keyframes** (`site/data/atlas-eras.json`, OpenHistoricalMap, CC0) and today, merged and sorted by date into **seven chapters** (ours: 218 BCE, 711, 1230, 1492, 1580, 1808, 1898) and eight regional tracks (Iberia and the Mediterranean, Atlantic and islands, North America and the Caribbean, South America, Africa, Asia, Oceania, worldwide). A record that tells an existing beat's story (`BEAT_ALIASES` in the parser: A78 ↔ Columbus I, A99 ↔ Magellan, A45 ↔ 1750…) becomes that stop's text; both ids deep-link (`#on-map-magellan`, `#on-map-A99`; the old `#on-map-open` goes to the 1492 keyframe). Every milestone of the sixty hangs from the stop that names it, else the last stop at or before its year. Routes, dates and sources: `docs/research/atlas-voyages.md`.
- **The engine** (`lib/atlas-globe.ts`, Canvas 2D + d3-geo, no WebGL, like Prometheus): an orthographic globe shaded as a sphere (or a flat Equal Earth view), on a warm dark ground. With the fog layer on, the unknown is a burgundy-black fog cut away by a low-resolution mask (wakes, landfalls, each keyframe's jurisdictions; the Old World dim from the start); **the dark means "not yet in the surviving record", never empty land**. The camera flies between stops and follows ships; the reader's wheel, pinch and drag take it, "back to the story" or the next stop give it back. Performance: device-pixel ratio capped at 2 (1.5 on phones), coarser shapes and the 1:50m coast fetched only when zoomed in, frames skipped when drawing is slow.
- **Realms** (#104, Carlos: "you can't see how far Spain reaches, what is Portugal"): two sources, both OpenHistoricalMap. The **American keyframes** (`site/data/atlas-eras.json`, 1492–1898, one curated set per keyframe) and the **realms** (`tools/atlas-realms.json` → `site/data/atlas-realms.json` + `site/public/data/atlas-realms.topo.json`, built by `tools/build-atlas-eras.py --realms`): chains of dated relations for the peninsula from 218 BCE (Carthaginian Iberia, the Roman provinces, Sueves, Visigoths, Byzantine Spania, al-Andalus, the emirate and caliphate, Granada, Asturias, León, Castile, Pamplona/Navarre, Aragón, the Catalan counties, Majorca, Portugal), Spain and Portugal to today, and their holdings in Europe, Africa, Asia and the Pacific; each drawn between its own OHM dates (a bare-year end runs to the end of that year), narrowed never widened. **Where OHM has no shape, nothing is drawn and the card says so** (`gaps`: the taifas, Almoravids and Almohads 1031–1232; Portugal 1199–1297; Baetica before 14 BCE). Never use relations whose ways carry CC BY-SA (listed in the curation file); many Iberian ways are IGN/CNIG municipal limits under CC BY 4.0, credited on the page. Every realm drawn is filled in its shade, edged (a dark seam and a paper hairline), named where its name fits (the stop's own first, then by size; never under a panel) and named on hover. **The stop's territory is outlined in light**: a keyframe's changes, or the realms a record's places lie in (the smallest unit holding each place), which the cartouche, the explainer and the card name too. Today: the Spanish-speaking states red, the Portuguese-speaking teal, the US and the Philippines hatched, modern borders faint (only there).
- **Status by year** (#104, Carlos: Brazil in Portugal's colour in 1896): every realm is coloured and named by its status in the year shown, from `site/data/atlas-status.json` (curated: each family's type and sovereign, and dated periods where its status changed while its shape is on the globe, each with its source: Spanish America's juntas, wars and independences, Brazil 1815/1822/1889, the 1898 transfers, the Dominican re-annexation 1861–65, Cuba 1902, the Philippines 1946, the Republics of Spain and Portugal, the Iberian Union…). Shapes stay the keyframe's (a keyframe is drawn until the next one), names follow the date ("Republic of Colombia" in 1896, not New Granada). `npm run atlas:status` fails if a territory wears Spain's or Portugal's colour after it left them (36 dated transfers, including the African independences of 1968–76, Goa 1961 and Macau 1999), or a period lacks a source.
- **Click a territory** (#104): a popover beside the click (a sheet on phones; Esc, ×, a click elsewhere or a new stop close it) says what it was that year: its name then and today's country under the pointer, its status in plain words, its sovereign, its capital (Wikidata P36 for that date, else nothing), the period, the OHM relation of its border (and "drawn as in the 1848 keyframe" when the shape is older than the year), the source, and the stop most worth reading. Grey land says only "Outside this Atlas's story" and today's country. The facts come from `tools/build-atlas-status.py` (OHM tags, Wikidata capitals and ISO codes, all CC0) into `site/public/data/atlas-units.json`, fetched on the first click. Keyboard: Layers lists every realm on the globe now; choosing one turns the globe to it and opens the same popover.
- Colours: the map palette above (`site/lib/atlas-palette.ts`); events as burgundy lozenges; with "recorded" on, wakes and small lights at the landfalls (they never wash the realms). The interface around it is the brand's dark glass with a paper legend. Styles in `app/[lang]/atlas/atlas.css`.
- **Layout** (#104, Carlos: "panels that slide under others"): the left column (cartouche, then the legend at its foot; the sentence gives way first on short windows, then the legend folds to a chip, and it is always in Layers too), the rail and scale right of the map, the card right of everything, the time bar at the foot; the index and the layers open as drawers in the left column's place. The explainer never sits on a panel (it waits instead). `tools/atlas-layout-check.mjs` measures every floating piece at 13 viewports (1280×720 to 2560×1440, 1024×768, 768×1024, 390×844, zoom 125%) in both languages and fails on any overlap or clipping: run it before changing the layout. The explainer says where (the places, and the realm they lay in); that places are approximate is said once, in the legend.
- The app (`components/atlas/AtlasStage.tsx`): a welcome over the turning planet (begin, jump to 1492, browse every stop, how to read the globe); Places & events (every stop, searchable, follow one region, and links to the text version); the card with **Previous / Next stop**; "Surprise me". The explainer (`explainer.ts`) lays a note beside what the stop changes; card and globe point at each other (landfalls, jurisdictions, treaty lines, a record's places), and clicking an event pin goes to its stop. **Reduced motion**: no playback, no flights, no inertia; every step shows the stop finished. **Fail open**: without JavaScript the app says so and links to `/[lang]/atlas/chronicle` (`components/atlas/Chronicle.tsx`), whose "Show on the globe" links open the app at that stop.

- **HGIS de las Indias is reference only** (non-commercial licence); historical-basemaps is not used (GPL, errors). Data sources logged in `assets/art/SOURCES.md`.
