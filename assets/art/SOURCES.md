Prompt (verbatim):

> A copperplate line engraving in the style of an 18th-century architectural treatise plate (like the plates of Vignola's "Regola delle cinque ordini"), printed in a single dark burgundy ink (#470B15) on warm cream laid paper (#F9F3DD). Strictly two colours: burgundy ink and cream paper. No other hues, no gold, no grey, no colour washes. Subject: a perfectly frontal, orthographic, symmetrical elevation of a Doric portico, matching the attached reference image exactly in layout and proportions: three Roman Doric columns after Vignola, with gentle entasis, standing on a stylobate of three wide steps. Each column has a simple Attic base on a square plinth and a Doric capital (square abacus, rounded echinus, three thin annulets). The column shafts are SMOOTH, NOT fluted, shaded with fine vertical engraved hatching that is dense at both edges and fades to almost bare paper down the central third of each shaft, which must stay light and clean. Above them, a full Doric entablature spanning the width: a plain architrave (a clean, empty horizontal band), a frieze of seven triglyphs and six square metopes (each column centred exactly under the 2nd, 4th and 6th triglyph counting from the left), small guttae under each triglyph, and a cornice with mutules and a crisp moulded profile. The six metopes are EMPTY: plain paper inside a fine engraved frame. The architrave band is EMPTY. Rendering: confident burin lines, cross-hatching only in the deepest shadows, light falling from the upper left with soft engraved shadows under the cornice, under the capitals and to the right of each column. Fine, crisp, high-detail line work, like a museum-quality print. Subtle paper grain, a faint plate mark around the image. Absolutely NO text, letters, numbers, inscriptions, captions, signatures, monograms or watermarks anywhere. No people, no sky, no landscape, no background buildings, no pediment, no ornament or sculpture inside the metopes. Plain cream paper around the portico with even margins. Aspect ratio 5:4, highest resolution.

(Gemini only offered 4:3; that is what was used.)
