# The Atlas's voyages: routes, dates and sources

Issue #71, 2026-10-29. The voyages sailed on the rebuilt Atlas (`site/data/atlas-voyages.json`), how their routes were drawn, and what they rest on.

## How a route is made

- Each voyage is a list of **waypoints**. Named waypoints are documented places with the date the voyage's own account gives (landfall, anchorage, departure). Unnamed waypoints only keep the line at sea (round a cape, out into the Atlantic arc, along a coast); they carry no claim.
- Positions are the modern coordinates of the documented places, rounded to about a tenth of a degree. Where a place's identification is debated, the record says so (Guanahani; San Pablo in the Pacific).
- Between waypoints the line is a great circle, densified every 0.6°. It is **drawn, not logged**: the cards and the credit line say so.
- Dates are ISO; a month alone (`1500-05`, `1522-05`) where the day is uncertain. While a ship sails between two dated places the page shows only the month, never an invented day.
- The fog lifts along the wake (about 3° either side at sea, less on rivers and on foot) and around each dated landfall. The Old World is dim from the start (known to Iberians by chart or by report); the Americas and the Pacific are dark until reached. After the voyages the dark thins over the centuries (a visual convention, not a claim) and the border keyframes take over.

## The layouts

- **Atlantic chart** (Equal Earth centred on 30° W): the first voyages, the Americas and the border keyframes, today.
- **Globe** (orthographic, following the ship): the circumnavigation, the one voyage a flat chart cannot hold without cutting it.
- **Pacific chart** (Equal Earth centred on 170° W): the galleon and the tornaviaje, and the Philippines keyframe (1565–1583).
