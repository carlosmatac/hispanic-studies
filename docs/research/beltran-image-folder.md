# Beltrán's image folder ("Imágenes Intro YouTube")

Beltrán shared this Drive folder after the first meeting ([transcript](../meeting/2026-10-14-first-meeting-transcript.md), 00:59: "tengo una carpeta de muchísimos cuadros barrocos"). It is the image bank he uses for his YouTube intros.

- **Source:** <https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1NNQEQe-rYpxT50xFZsWo2Z2BDHyfGylH> (public link, last modified 25 Nov 2025)
- **Contents:** 145 files in three subfolders: *Barroco* (65), *XIX-XX* (42), *fotografías* (38). 144 downloaded; `Barroco/03.jpg` is blocked by Drive (too many accesses) and was not reviewed.
- **Not copied into the repo.** Most files are anonymous web downloads (Pinterest/X hashes) with no source. Before a proposal uses one, find the original (Commons, museum), save it in `assets/art/` and log it in `assets/art/SOURCES.md`.
- Identifications below are visual and unverified. IDs (`barr-01`…) follow alphabetical file order inside each subfolder. ★ = public domain (pre-1900 art) **and** ≥1800 px wide, i.e. usable as a full-bleed hero once the original is sourced.

## What it tells us

1. **The Baroque folder is about New Spain and Peru, not Spain.** Guadalupe (≈12 images), castas, biombos, the Mexico City Plaza Mayor, Indian nobles, evangelisation, Cuzco school. Spanish Golden Age painting (Velázquez, Zurbarán) is almost absent. Our proposals lean on peninsular art; his imagination is virreinal and mestizo. This matches the three pillars in the meeting (mestizaje, catolicismo, lengua).
2. **Maps and allegories recur:** Tenochtitlan 1524, Acapulco 1628, Hondius world map, Mitla, *Europa regina*. This backs his request for the map + timeline of the Hispanist canon.
3. **The photo folder is the "militant" register he asked for:** Chicano movement, UFW and César Chávez, Brown Berets, Reies López Tijerina and Tierra Amarilla, the Cristeros, Pancho Villa, the Zapatistas, Tina Modotti. It is mostly 20th-century US Southwest and Mexico (his "Southwest / cowboys" setting). Guadalupe banners at protests tie it back to the Baroque folder.
4. **The XIX-XX folder bridges the two:** Spanish and Mexican academic history painting (Pradilla, Puebla, Madrazo, Jara, Obregón), Casimiro Castro's lithographs of Mexico, the Taos Society of Artists (Southwest), and Chicano posters.
5. **Missing:** almost nothing for his second setting (Caribbean / Miami / neon). Albizu Campos (`xix-38`) is the only Caribbean item. If we want that setting, we need to ask him for material or source it ourselves.

## Rights flags

- **Copyrighted, do not use without a licence:** all press photos (`foto-01` NYT/UFW, `foto-20` AP, `foto-25`, EZLN shots), Chicano murals and posters (`foto-03`, `xix-11`, `xix-35`, `xix-39`), Diego Rivera and O'Gorman (`xix-03`, `xix-07`), recent paintings and illustrations (`xix-15`, `xix-30`, `xix-42`), Jesús Helguera-style calendar art (`xix-06`), the fan collage `barr-01`, and the *Pelayos* 1938 cover (`xix-02`, check). Modotti (d. 1942) is likely public domain in Mexico/EU but must be checked.
- **Watermarked:** `barr-63` (necesitasunhobby.com).
- **Low resolution (<1000 px), fine for thumbnails only:** 12 Barroco, 19 photos, 9 XIX-XX.
- **Visual duplicates:** `foto-30` = `foto-32`, `xix-04` ≈ `xix-12`.
- **Sensitive, ask before using:** `barr-14` Rizi, *Auto de fe* (1680); `barr-54` three-faced Trinity; `foto-20` Evo Morales gives Pope Francis the hammer-and-sickle crucifix; `foto-25` Rajoy and Xi.
