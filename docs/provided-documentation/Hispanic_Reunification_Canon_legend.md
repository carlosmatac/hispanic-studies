# Hispanic Reunification Canon — Classification and Reading Key

## Governing proposition

The Iberian worlds once possessed several overlapping forms of material and spiritual connection. Their political disaggregation did not end the question of unity. It generated rival projects of **restoration, recomposition, transfiguration, refoundation, and rearticulation**, each based on a different account of what the historical whole had been, what remained of it, and what kind of unity ought to be created.

“Hispanic” is used in the broad historical sense of the civilizations and political communities formed through the Iberian worlds. Portuguese, Brazilian, Lusophone African, Asian, Indigenous, Black Atlantic, Equatoguinean, and Filipino traditions remain historically distinct. Their presence in the chart does not imply their assent to a Hispanic or Iberophone project.

## Classification field adapted from Gustavo Bueno

The vertical field adapts the alternatives in Gustavo Bueno's **“España y América” (2001)**. It classifies the conception of unity presupposed by a concrete text, speech, institution, movement, or phase. It does not rank authors morally.

| Code | Status | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Bueno alternative | **Nationalist alternative.** The sovereign nation is the primary political reality; wider Hispanic, Latin American, Iberian, or Lusophone unity is secondary or superstructural. |
| A2 | Bueno alternative | **Inter-nationalist alternative.** A universal doctrine or institution not generated by the Iberian-American historical field supplies the principle of unity. |
| B1 | Bueno alternative | **American / Latin American / Indo-American whole.** America is treated as an attributive historical whole and the point of departure for a new project. |
| B2.4 | Bueno alternative | **Pan-American alternative.** The Hispanic and Portuguese American republics are parts of a greater hemispheric system. |
| B2.5 | Bueno alternative | **Occidentalist alternative.** The societies of the Iberian worlds are parts of Western civilization. |
| B2.6 | Bueno alternative | **Hispanist alternative.** Spain, Spanish America, and other historically connected societies are parts of a greater Hispanic Community. |
| B2.7 | Controlled extension | **Lusophone alternative.** Portuguese-speaking societies are parts of a historically constituted Lusophone community; this is an extension beyond Bueno's numbered alternatives. |
| B2.8 | Controlled extension | **Iberian / Iberophone alternative.** Hispanic and Lusophone communities are parts of a larger pluricontinental Iberian-derived whole; this is an extension beyond Bueno's numbered alternatives. |

The early-modern prologue—Charles V's Universal Monarchy, Philip II/I's Iberian Union, and the contested universalisms of Salamanca and the Fifth Empire tradition—is a **historical terminus a quo**, not a modern Bueno category.

## Forms of unity

- **I — isological:** similarity or a shared identity of language, religion, legal inheritance, origin, literary tradition, race/mixture, or political experience.
- **S — synalogical:** actual relations among parts, including institutions, law, commerce, diplomacy, congresses, universities, periodicals, migration, alliances, or organizations.
- **I→S — attempted reunification:** an effort to turn a shared or newly formulated identity into organized relations.

## Five operative modes

These are not additional Bueno alternatives. They identify what the concrete item does with its presumed unity. A single item may perform more than one operation.

| Mode | Definition | Visual form |
|---|---|---|
| **Restoration** | Reconstructs a previous synalogical order, or its governing identity of project, as the model of renewed unity. | Backward memory arc followed by a forward projection. |
| **Recomposition** | Organizes constituted political units into a new synalogical whole while preserving their recognizable identities. | Separate lines converge on a federal, confederal, congressional, or allied node. |
| **Transfiguration** | Transfers the material identity of unity from dynasty and sovereignty toward language, religion, law, literature, culture, memory, or civilizational purpose. | Diagonal broken trajectory across political and cultural fields. |
| **Refoundation** | Reorganizes inherited historical materials as a new attributive whole under a new American, Indo-American, popular, socialist, anti-imperialist, or postcolonial project. | Forward trajectory arising from B1 rather than returning to a metropolitan center. |
| **Rearticulation** | Renews synalogical relations among independent parts without requiring one federation, state, or sovereign authority. | Linked constellation among institutions and independent political centers. |
